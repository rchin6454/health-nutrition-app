## Fridge and freezer temperatures

A fridge should be at 4 °C or colder and a freezer at −18 °C. Home fridges are often warmer
than people think, especially when overfilled or opened often in hot weather, so a cheap fridge
thermometer is useful. Keep the coldest shelves for milk, curd, paneer, meat and fish, and not
the door. Freezing stops bacteria from growing but does not kill them, so food can spoil again
once thawed.

## Cooling cooked food quickly

Large pots of dal, curry or rice cool slowly, and the centre can stay warm for hours. Divide
hot food into small, shallow containers so it cools quickly, and put it in the fridge within
2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour in hot weather. It is fine to put warm food in the fridge;
waiting for it to cool completely on the counter is riskier.

## Reheating leftovers

Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot all the way through (74 °C); bring dals, curries
and gravies to a rolling boil. Stir while reheating in a microwave so there are no cold spots.
Reheat only the portion you will eat, and do not reheat the same food more than once. Reheating
does not make food safe if it was left out too long, because some bacteria make toxins that
heat does not destroy.

## Thawing frozen food safely

Thaw frozen meat, chicken and fish in the fridge, in a leak-proof bag in cold water changed
every 30 minutes, or in the microwave. Food thawed in cold water or the microwave should be
cooked straight away. Never thaw meat on the kitchen counter: the outside warms up into the
danger zone while the centre is still frozen.

## Power cuts and the fridge

During a power cut, keep the fridge and freezer doors closed. A closed fridge keeps food cold
for about 4 hours; a full freezer holds its temperature for about 48 hours, or 24 hours if it
is half full. After a long power cut, throw away perishable food such as milk, curd, paneer,
cooked food, meat, fish, eggs and cut fruit that has been warmer than 4 °C for more than
2 hours. Never taste food to check whether it is safe.

## When in doubt, throw it out

Spoiled food does not always look or smell bad, and food that smells fine can still make you
ill. If you are not sure how long a perishable food has been out of the fridge, or whether it
was stored safely, it is safer to throw it away.
