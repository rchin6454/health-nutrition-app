## A balanced plate

A balanced Indian meal includes a variety of foods: cereals and millets, pulses and legumes,
vegetables (including green leafy vegetables), fruits, milk or curd, and a small amount of nuts,
seeds and oil. Vegetables and fruits should make up about half of what you eat in a day. Using
a variety of foods from different groups helps meet the need for protein, vitamins, minerals
and fibre without relying on supplements.

## Whole grains and millets

Whole grains and millets such as ragi, jowar, bajra, and whole wheat keep the bran and germ,
which carry most of the fibre, B vitamins and minerals. Refined grains such as maida and highly
polished rice lose much of these during milling. Replacing part of the refined cereal in meals
with whole grains or millets adds fibre and helps keep blood sugar steadier.

## Protein from vegetarian diets

Pulses, dals, beans, peas, milk, curd, paneer, nuts and seeds are the main protein sources in
vegetarian diets. Eating cereals and pulses together, as in dal-chawal, khichdi, idli or
roti with dal, gives a better mix of amino acids than either alone. Sprouting and fermenting
pulses and grains improves how well their nutrients are digested and absorbed.

## Salt, sugar and fat

Limit salt to less than 5 g a day (about one level teaspoon), counting salt in pickles, papad,
namkeen and packaged foods. Keep added sugar low, including sugar in tea, sweets and soft
drinks. Use a variety of cooking oils in moderation, and avoid reheating the same oil for frying
again and again. Deep-fried snacks and sweets are best kept for occasional treats.

## Ultra-processed foods and labels

Ultra-processed foods such as chips, biscuits, instant noodles, sugary drinks and many ready
snacks tend to be high in fat, sugar and salt and low in fibre. Eat them rarely. When buying
packaged food, read the nutrition label and ingredient list, and compare products per 100 g
for sugar, salt and fat rather than trusting front-of-pack claims.

## Water and activity

Drink enough safe water through the day, more in hot weather and during physical work or
exercise. Regular physical activity, together with a balanced diet, helps maintain a healthy
body weight and lowers the risk of diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease.
