## Iron and its absorption

Plant foods such as green leafy vegetables, pulses, millets like bajra and ragi, and jaggery
contain non-haem iron, which the body absorbs less easily than the haem iron in meat, chicken
and fish. Eating vitamin C-rich foods with the meal (lemon, amla, guava, tomato, citrus fruits)
improves iron absorption. Tea and coffee reduce it, so they are best avoided for about an hour
before and after meals. Cooking in iron utensils can add a little iron to food.

## Calcium and bones

Milk, curd, paneer and ragi are good sources of calcium in Indian diets; sesame seeds (til) and
some green leafy vegetables also contribute. Vitamin D, made in the skin in sunlight, helps the
body absorb calcium. Regular weight-bearing activity helps keep bones strong.

## Fibre

Fibre comes from whole grains, millets, pulses, vegetables, fruits, nuts and seeds. It helps
digestion and keeps you full for longer, and soluble fibre can help lower blood cholesterol and
slow the rise in blood sugar after meals. Whole fruits give more fibre than fruit juices.

## Cooking to keep nutrients

Wash vegetables before cutting, not after, and cut them into large pieces just before cooking.
Cook them in a small amount of water with a lid, and use the leftover cooking water in dal or
gravy instead of throwing it away. Pressure cooking and steaming keep more vitamins than long
boiling. Do not wash rice many times or cook it in excess water that is then drained. Avoid
adding baking soda to dals and vegetables, as it destroys some B vitamins.

## Soaking, sprouting and fermenting

Soaking pulses and grains before cooking shortens cooking time and reduces compounds that block
mineral absorption. Sprouting increases vitamin C and B vitamins and makes pulses easier to
digest. Fermented foods such as idli, dosa, dhokla and curd are easier to digest and can improve
the availability of some nutrients.

## Rice, roti and blood sugar

White rice and refined flour raise blood sugar faster than whole grains, millets and brown rice,
which contain more fibre. Combining cereals with dal, vegetables, curd or salad in the same meal
slows the rise in blood sugar. Portion size matters as much as the choice of grain. People with
diabetes should get a meal plan from a doctor or registered dietitian.
