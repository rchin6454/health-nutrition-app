## Leftover rice and the heat

Cooked rice can carry spores of Bacillus cereus that survive boiling. If the rice is left at
room temperature, the spores grow and can produce a toxin that causes vomiting within a few
hours, and reheating or frying the rice does not destroy it. In Indian summers, when kitchens
are often above 32 °C, cooked rice, dal and curries should go into the fridge within 1 hour.
Rice left out overnight should be thrown away.

## Milk and dairy

Boil loose milk before use and refrigerate it after it cools. Packet milk is pasteurised, but
once opened it is as perishable as boiled milk. Milk, curd, paneer, khoya and milk sweets spoil
quickly in the heat; do not keep them out of the fridge for more than 1–2 hours. Milk that has
curdled on its own, smells sour, or has been left out during a long power cut should be
discarded.

## Monsoon precautions

Bacteria and moulds grow faster in the warm, humid monsoon, and water contamination is more
common. Drink boiled or purified water, wash vegetables well (especially leafy greens), cook
food thoroughly, and eat it fresh. Store grains, flours and spices in dry, airtight containers
and throw them away if they show mould or insects.

## Buying food safely

Buy perishable food last and take it home quickly. Choose fish and meat kept on ice or chilled,
with a fresh smell. Check packaged foods for an intact seal, the FSSAI licence number, and the
use-by or best-before date. Avoid dented, bulging or leaking cans and packets.

## Washing fruits and vegetables

Wash fruits and vegetables under clean running water just before using them, rubbing the
surface. Soaking them for a few minutes in clean water before rinsing helps remove dirt. Do not
use soap or detergent on produce. Wash melons and other fruit even if you do not eat the rind,
because a knife carries dirt from the rind into the flesh.

## Food adulteration checks

Some adulterants can be spotted at home, though a lab test is needed to be sure. A drop of pure
milk on a slanted, polished surface flows slowly and leaves a white trail, while milk diluted
with water runs off quickly without a trace. Milk that forms a thick lather when shaken with
an equal amount of water may contain detergent. A few drops of iodine turning milk, paneer or
khoya blue suggest added starch. Buy from trusted sources and check packaged foods for the
FSSAI logo and licence number.
